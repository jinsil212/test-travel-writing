## 설명란 (원문, 링크·크레딧 줄 제외)

> Spacecraft have reached Mars, Saturn, Pluto, and even the edge of interstellar space. But there is one direction we have barely explored: straight above and below the Sun.
> This video explains why escaping vertically from the solar system is far harder than it sounds. Earth already moves around the Sun at about 67,000 mph, and every spacecraft we launch inherits that sideways motion. To go directly “up” out of the ecliptic plane, a probe must fight that orbital velocity a problem that makes reaching a point above the Sun more difficult than flying to Pluto.
> We explore the mystery of the Sun’s poles, the Ulysses mission, Solar Orbiter’s first images of the solar south pole, the strange shape of the heliosphere, and why scientists are considering extreme mission concepts like solar sails and the solar Oberth maneuver.
> To climb above the Sun, a spacecraft may first need to fall toward it.
